## 4. Variants and conditional topics

- Core English variants: Arabic band / Arab band / Arabic wedding musicians / Middle Eastern wedding band / live oriental music. Lead with “Arabic” and “Middle Eastern”; retain “oriental” as a secondary description of the repertoire.
- Zaffa variants to monitor: zaffa, zaffe, zaffeh, zaffah. They belong to one topic; they do not need separate pages.
- Instrument variants: qanun / kanun; buzuk / bouzouki (confirm which instrument is intended); darbuka / doumbek. Keep the actual instrument names accurate.
- Dabke / dabka / دبكة: use music terms such as “live dabke music for weddings” and “موسيقى دبكة للأعراس” only if offered. A dance troupe is a different booking expectation.
- Syrian wedding band, Lebanese wedding band, Palestinian wedding music, Levantine wedding music; فرقة أعراس سورية, أغاني أعراس لبنانية, موسيقى أعراس فلسطينية: useful only when Ahmad confirms the repertoire and experience. Do not claim a nationality or specialty based solely on the audience.
- Oud cocktail-hour music, Arabic restaurant live music, corporate Arabic entertainment, bilingual wedding MC: confirm availability before targeting them as services.
- “Arabic DJ,” “wedding photographer,” “dabke dancers,” and “Islamic nasheed wedding” should not be service targets unless those services are actually offered. An honest band-versus-DJ planning article can address the comparison query.
- Avoid “best,” “#1,” “cheap,” and invented price claims. They are unnecessary for matching booking intent.

## 7. Implementation priorities that support these keywords

1. **Make Arabic crawlable at its own URLs.** The current code starts in English, prerenders English, and loads Arabic through a client-side toggle/localStorage. Arabic is not independently addressable. This limits reliable discovery of the Arabic version; it does not establish that Google has indexed none of it. Add prerendered Arabic URLs, ordinary language-switch links, self-referencing canonicals, and reciprocal English/Arabic `hreflang` annotations. Google recommends separate URLs for language versions. [Google multilingual guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites)
2. **Add useful service/location pages.** Each should have service details, actual travel coverage, original performance evidence where available, relevant questions, and a clear booking contact. Google lists doorway abuse and keyword stuffing in its spam policies; avoid city-swap pages and giant public keyword lists. [Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
3. **Support local discovery.** Keep Google Business Profile information accurate, describe services and real service areas, and earn honest reviews and relevant venue/community links. Google's local ranking guidance identifies relevance, distance, and prominence; keywords alone do not guarantee map visibility across every city. [Google local ranking guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)
4. **Publish original useful articles.** Show the band's own practical experience and media. A planning guide should answer the question without requiring a booking inquiry for every detail. [Google helpful-content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
5. **Measure and revise.** In Search Console, compare impressions, clicks, CTR, average position, and landing pages for the English and Arabic clusters. Track qualified inquiries by landing page separately. Establish a baseline and review after publication; 8–12 weeks is a practical initial review window, not a ranking deadline. Use Keyword Planner or another volume tool if numeric demand estimates are needed.
